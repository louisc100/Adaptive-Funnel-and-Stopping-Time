This report is educational research, not personal investment advice. Volatility harvesting works best with a basket of liquid, volatile names that have durable business narratives and enough quality that buying dips is not just a hope trade.

\section*{Bottom line}

Strongest current candidates for a volatility-harvesting style approach:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item AMD
  \item NVDA
  \item PLTR
  \item TSLA
\end{enumerate}

Higher-risk tactical satellites:

\begin{itemize}
  \item 5. COIN
  \item 6. MSTR
\end{itemize}

If the goal is steady harvesting rather than maximum drama, the cleaner basket is AMD, NVDA, and PLTR, with TSLA at a smaller weight.

\section*{Why these names}

\subsection*{AMD}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Reported record Q4 2025 revenue of \$10.3 billion and strong full-year results.
  \item Next major catalyst is Q1 2026 earnings on May 5, 2026.
  \item As of April 24, 2026, shares had surged enough to push market cap above \$500 billion for the first time.
\end{itemize}

Why it fits: big AI narrative, sharp earnings moves, deep liquidity, and a real business foundation. Main risk: valuation after a very strong April run.

\subsection*{NVDA}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Fiscal Q4 2026 revenue was \$68.1 billion, up 73\% year over year.
  \item Data center revenue was \$62.3 billion.
  \item On April 24, 2026, NVDA closed around \$208.26, its first record close since October 2025.
\end{itemize}

Why it fits: strongest core AI franchise, excellent liquidity, and frequent sentiment swings around a very strong fundamental trend. Main risk: may swing less violently than the more speculative names.

\subsection*{PLTR}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Q1 2026 earnings are scheduled for May 4, 2026.
  \item Most recent reported quarter was very strong: Q4 2025 revenue near \$1.41 billion, up about 70\% year over year.
  \item Shares were still well below the late-2025 peak as of April 24, 2026, leaving room for continued two-way moves.
\end{itemize}

Why it fits: strong AI software narrative, sharp valuation debates, and real contract-backed momentum. Main risk: multiple compression if growth cools.

\subsection*{TSLA}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Tesla released Q1 2026 financial results on April 22, 2026.
  \item Recent reporting around that release put revenue near \$22.4 billion.
  \item The stock closed at \$387.51 on April 22, 2026 ahead of earnings and remains one of the market's most actively traded large caps.
\end{itemize}

Why it fits: huge liquidity, repeated narrative overreactions, and many catalysts. Main risk: the story can overwhelm near-term fundamentals for long stretches.

\subsection*{COIN}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Q1 2026 earnings are scheduled for May 7, 2026.
  \item The stock remains closely linked to crypto prices, trading volume, and regulatory developments.
  \item Around April 24, 2026, COIN was trading roughly around \$214 to \$215 while Bitcoin was around \$77,000 to \$78,000.
\end{itemize}

Why it fits: very high volatility and fast snapbacks. Main risk: it is effectively a second-order crypto bet.

\subsection*{MSTR}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Strategy reported Q4 2025 results on February 5, 2026 and disclosed holdings of 713,502 BTC as of February 1, 2026.
  \item Q1 2026 earnings are scheduled for May 5, 2026.
  \item Around April 24, 2026, MSTR was trading in the mid \$160s.
\end{itemize}

Why it fits: extreme tradable volatility. Main risk: it layers financing and capital-structure risk on top of Bitcoin volatility.

\section*{Suggested basket structure}

One practical way to express the idea:

\begin{itemize}
  \item 30\% NVDA
  \item 25\% AMD
  \item 20\% PLTR
  \item 15\% TSLA
  \item 7\% COIN
  \item 3\% MSTR
\end{itemize}

This is not a recommendation to use those exact weights. It is a way to keep the most violent names from dominating the basket.

\section*{How to harvest the volatility}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Rebalance on fixed bands, not on mood. For example, trim when a name rises 20\% to 25\% above target weight and add when it falls 20\% to 25\% below.
  \item Treat earnings carefully because gap risk can overwhelm normal mean reversion.
  \item Keep dry powder. The method breaks when you cannot add on sharp down moves.
  \item Use a basket rather than one name so winners can fund laggards.
  \item Watch correlation, especially between COIN and MSTR.
\end{itemize}
